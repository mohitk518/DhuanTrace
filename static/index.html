Hidden hotspots · pollution the models can't explain

Every CPCB station and citizen sensor is compared with what the CAMS model plus traced farm-fire smoke expects. Places far above the rest point to a local source: a factory, burning garbage, dust. Citizen reports, nearby industry and NASA's satellite layers for aerosol and NO₂ are added as evidence. In the satellite pictures the circle is the spot; the app compares it with the ring around it. These are daily daytime views of the whole air column, not ground readings.

Federated network · separate nodes that share only weights

Four separate programs, each with its own process, port and memory, stand in for the regions and neighbouring states of this node (named in the table). Each keeps its own air-quality history. The coordinator sends out the shared model; each node trains on its own data and sends back five numbers. Every message is signed, and any reply carrying anything beyond the protocol's fields is refused.

Report smoke or burning

Gemini reads the photo, names the smoke source and adds it to the map. Evening reports fill the gap the satellites leave. 🔒 Your photo and its rough location (about 1 km) appear on the public map. No name or phone number is asked or stored. Please keep faces and vehicle number plates out of the photo.

Data: NASA FIRMS (VIIRS S-NPP, NOAA-20, NOAA-21, MODIS) · CPCB real-time AQI via data.gov.in, with the World Air Quality Index Project (aqicn.org) as automatic backup · Open-Meteo hourly wind and CAMS air-quality forecast · Google Gemini for photo reading and alerts. Method: smoke from every fire and every citizen report is followed forward on the hourly wind field for 36 hours; paths passing within 30 km of a place count toward it. AQI uses CPCB NAQI breakpoints. Screening-level estimates for decision support, not an official forecast.
